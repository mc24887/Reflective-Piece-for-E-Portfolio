Module Overview

This e-portfolio documents my progress across Units 1–12 in Research Methods & Professional Practice. It includes ethical analyses, collaborative discussion work, a literature review (Unit 7, summative), proposal preparation, statistical activities, and visual analysis. Each unit entry highlights key submissions, evidence, reflection, and skills developed. The portfolio is now complete and ready for final assessment.

Unit 1 – Scientific Investigation & Ethics

Key Submissions
  • Initial Forum Post – ACM Case Study (Inadequate Security Measures): Applied ACM 1.2 (avoid harm) and 2.9 (secure systems); compared with BCS public-interest and integrity duties; noted potential Data Protection Act 2018 implications.

Unit 3 – Methodology & Research Methods

Key Submissions
  • Method/Design Map: Qualitative, quantitative, mixed methods; exploratory vs descriptive design; primary vs secondary data choices linked to the project.
Reflection

Matching methods to research questions (not the reverse) improved rigour. Early consideration of validity, reliability and generalisability reduced later rework.

Skills Developed
  • Method selection
  • Design rationale
  • Risk/assumption awareness

Unit 4 – Case Studies, Focus Groups & Observation

Key Submissions
  • Methods Overview: Case studies (depth, limited generalisability), focus groups (6–10 participants with common background), quantitative/qualitative observation (objective measures vs natural behaviour).
  • Applied Ethics Scenario (Privacy): Access control, consent and data minimisation when contacting individuals via records data.
Reflection

No single method is perfect. Combining methods (e.g., observation + focus group) improves triangulation; consent and governance remain central.

Skills Developed
  • Method comparison
  • Ethical analysis in data collection
  • Triangulation planning

Unit 5 – Interviews, Surveys & Questionnaire Design

Data Collection Update

Mixed-methods plan: survey (quantitative breadth) + semi-structured interviews (qualitative depth). Piloting, transparent consent and data minimisation embedded.

Wiki – Questionnaire Design
  • Clear, unbiased wording; one construct per item
  • Direct alignment with objectives; pilot before deployment
  • Ethics: purpose limitation, consent, retention/deletion
Reflective Activity 2 – Inappropriate Use of Surveys

Analysis of Cambridge Analytica (2018): purpose drift, opaque permissions and misuse of data for targeting. Impacts: ethical (consent), social (trust/democracy), legal (GDPR/DPA), professional (ACM/BCS). Commitments: proportional data, explicit consent, DPIA where appropriate.

Skills Developed
  • Instrument design
  • Survey ethics & governance
  • Mixed-methods integration

Unit 7 – Hypothesis Testing (Inferential Statistics) Summative

Applied paired and independent t-tests; framed null/alternative hypotheses; verified assumptions before inference.

Interpretation: Design 1 outperforms Design 2; Diet A outperforms Diet B. Results reported with p-values and considered alongside variability/effect size.

Unit 8 – Data Analysis & Visualisation

Visual analytics were used to present and validate results from Units 6–7. Histograms supported distribution checks; boxplots clarified spread/IQR differences (e.g., narrower for Diet A); scatterplots revealed relationships. Visual integrity (consistent axes, accurate labels) ensured transparency.

Interpretation: Histograms confirmed approximate normality and boxplots showed Diet A’s tighter spread and higher median. These visuals validated statistical findings and increased confidence in subsequent inference.

Skills Developed
  • Chart design (histograms/boxplots/scatterplots)
  • Linking visuals to tests
  • Ethical presentation (no distorted scales)

Unit 9 – Data Analysis (Advanced)

An extended inferential analysis included correlation/regression and nonparametric alternatives when assumptions failed. Results include sample sizes and effect sizes to contextualise significance.

Interpretation: Reporting n and effect sizes with p-values clarifies both statistical and practical significance. Visual checks (boxplots, scatter) corroborate test outcomes and enhance decision relevance.

Unit 10 – Research Proposal Presentation Summative

Delivered a 15-minute narrated presentation on Generative AI Risks in Software Security and Email Workflows. The proposal combined PRISMA-guided synthesis with an evaluation rubric aligned to NIST AI RMF and ENISA (2024).

Tutor feedback reflection: Feedback confirmed strong domain knowledge, critical comparison of offensive and defensive AI, and exemplary structure. To refine further, I will discuss PRISMA’s limits in technical domains and expand evaluation of rubric generalisability. Visual storytelling (radar chart, PRISMA diagram) will be enhanced.

  • Evidence: narrated slides and transcript.

Unit 12 – Final e-Portfolio Submission Completed

The final submission collates artefacts and reflections across Units 1–11. The separate 1 000-word reflective report (submitted to Moodle) provides the full narrative; the summary below highlights key outcomes.

Reflective Study Summary

This module integrated methodological rigour with ethical accountability. I progressed from a technical view of research to an iterative, reflective practice supported by PRISMA-based synthesis, inferential analysis, and ethical framing (ACM/BCS; GDPR/DPA). The Generative AI project consolidated statistical and governance skills through a rubric that connected threats, defences and policy requirements. Tutor feedback confirmed strengths in structure, criticality and communication; I have embedded improvements (visual evidence, effect sizes, limitations) throughout this e-Portfolio. My complete artefacts are accessible via my e-Portfolio.
